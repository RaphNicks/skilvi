Why we built this

Global platforms work against locals

USD payouts, delayed transfers, arbitrary account freezes — and competing against the whole world on price. A great PH developer or a great Lagos electrician shouldn't have to fight that fight.

Local work runs on WhatsApp

No profiles, no reviews, no payment protection. If the tiler disappears after your deposit, there is no one to call. Informal trust is real — but it doesn't scale.

Clients can't tell who's real

Whether it's a logo or a bathroom, the risk feels the same: you've never met this person. So deals stall, or money moves off-platform, unprotected.

How we keep both sides safe

Escrow on every order

Clients pay Skilvi, not the worker. Money is released only when the work is done and approved.

Verified identities

A one-time check of government ID + photo. The badge says who they are — never what we certified.

Real dispute mediation

Frozen funds, evidence, message history, and a 5-business-day resolution target.

Audited, accountable staff

Every admin action on money or accounts is written to an immutable audit log.

Simple money rules

Workers never pay to exist on Skilvi. We make money when you both succeed.

Profiles & job posts — free 10% commission per completed order Verification ₦5,000 once (2 years) Promotion — optional, always labelled

Where we're going

Phase 1 · Nigeria (now)

One city at a time: Port Harcourt, Lagos, Abuja. Digital + trades supply, escrow, verification, promotions.

Phase 2 · West Africa

Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya pilots: local languages, local rails, same trust model. Workers already serving cross-border clients.

Phase 3 · The world

International clients hiring African skills with confidence — identity verified, payments protected, reputation portable. Plus the Learn engine: courses that turn interest into billable skill.

The team

Raphael Zidougha

Founder, Skilvi

I started Skilvi because I kept seeing the same problem: people can be genuinely good at something and still struggle to find people willing to trust them enough to pay for it.

I wanted to build something that could change that.

Skilvi is my attempt to make skills more useful in the real world — whether someone is a developer, designer, plumber, electrician, tailor, painter, or just someone who knows how to do something valuable. You shouldn't need to know the “right people” before your skill can become an opportunity.

I'm building Skilvi from Nigeria, with the hope that it eventually becomes something much bigger than a marketplace.

There's still a lot to figure out, and we're definitely not pretending we've figured it all out. But the goal is simple: help people discover skills, learn, and earn from what they can do.

That's what Skilvi means to me.

When I'm not working on Skilvi or coding or debugging, I'm usually gaming or watching movies 😁
